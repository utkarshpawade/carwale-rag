# Car glossary (general knowledge, not model-specific CarWale data)

## Mild hybrid vs strong hybrid vs plug-in hybrid
A mild hybrid (Maruti Suzuki markets its system as "Smart Hybrid"; others say 12V/48V mild hybrid or ISG) uses a small integrated starter-generator and battery to assist the petrol engine, enable smoother idle start-stop and recover braking energy. It cannot drive the car on electric power alone, so fuel savings are modest. A strong hybrid (also called self-charging or full hybrid, e.g. Toyota/Maruti "Intelligent Electric Hybrid", Honda e:HEV) has a bigger battery and motor that can drive the car on electricity alone at low speeds, which gives much higher mileage in city traffic; it never needs plugging in. A plug-in hybrid (PHEV) has an even larger battery that is charged from a socket and can run tens of kilometres on electricity before the engine starts.

## How a strong (self-charging) hybrid works
A strong hybrid pairs a petrol engine with one or more electric motors and a battery charged by the engine and by regenerative braking. In stop-go city traffic it often runs on electric power alone, so city mileage is far better than a petrol-only car, and it usually uses an e-CVT automatic gearbox. Benefits: high real-world efficiency, smooth and quiet drive, no charging needed. Trade-offs: higher purchase price than the petrol version and smaller boot in some cars because of the battery. Strong hybrids suit buyers with heavy city usage.

## Electric car basics: range, battery and charging
An EV's battery size is measured in kWh; a bigger battery generally means more range. Claimed range figures (ARAI/MIDC test cycle) are measured in lab conditions; real-world range is usually 20-30% lower depending on speed, AC use, temperature and driving style. AC charging (home wall box, 3-11 kW) typically takes 6-15 hours for a full charge, while DC fast charging (25-150 kW) can take the battery from 10-80% in roughly 30-60 minutes. EVs have very low running cost per km and minimal maintenance (no engine oil), but need access to charging.

## ADAS explained (Level 1 and Level 2)
ADAS (Advanced Driver Assistance Systems) use radar and cameras to help the driver. Level 1 ADAS offers single assists such as forward collision warning or automatic emergency braking (AEB). Level 2 ADAS combines functions like adaptive cruise control (keeps a set distance to the car ahead), lane keep assist / lane centring, AEB, blind spot monitoring, rear cross-traffic alert and high-beam assist. The driver must stay attentive with hands on the wheel; ADAS is assistance, not self-driving. Performance can be limited on poorly marked Indian roads.

## Transmission types: Manual, AMT, CVT, DCT, torque converter, iMT, e-CVT
Manual: cheapest, most control, tiring in traffic. AMT (automated manual): a manual gearbox with automatic actuation; affordable and efficient but shifts can feel jerky. CVT (continuously variable): smooth and seamless, good in the city, can feel rubber-band-like when pushed. DCT (dual-clutch): very quick shifts and sporty, common with turbo-petrol engines. Torque converter automatic (AT/TC): smooth and durable, common in bigger cars and diesels. iMT (intelligent manual): clutchless manual with a gear lever but no clutch pedal. e-CVT: the gearbox used in strong hybrids, behaves like a smooth CVT.

## Bharat NCAP vs Global NCAP crash ratings
NCAP (New Car Assessment Programme) crash tests rate cars from 0 to 5 stars for adult occupant protection (AOP) and child occupant protection (COP). Bharat NCAP is India's government-run programme (launched 2023), while Global NCAP is an independent programme that tested many Indian cars under its "Safer Cars for India" campaign. A 5-star rating means strong crash protection; ratings from different programmes or older test protocols are not directly comparable. Not every car has been crash tested.

## Ex-showroom price vs on-road price
The ex-showroom price is what the dealer charges for the car including GST. The on-road price adds RTO registration and road tax (which varies by state and fuel type), insurance, and sometimes TCS (1% on cars above Rs 10 lakh), FASTag and handling or accessory charges. On-road prices are typically 10-20% higher than ex-showroom, and EVs often get road-tax waivers in many states. Budget queries such as "under 10 lakh" usually refer to ex-showroom prices.

## Mileage terms: ARAI claimed vs real-world, kmpl vs km/kg vs km
ARAI (or ICAT) claimed mileage comes from a standardised lab test and is useful for comparing cars, but real-world mileage is usually lower. CarWale-tested figures come from its own city/highway drives, and owner-reported figures are averages shared by owners. Petrol and diesel efficiency is measured in kmpl (km per litre), CNG in km/kg, and electric cars in km of range per full charge.

## Petrol vs diesel vs CNG vs electric vs hybrid: which suits whom
Petrol: lower purchase price, refined, best for low to moderate running (under ~1,000-1,200 km a month). Diesel: higher torque and highway efficiency, suits high running and highway use, but costs more upfront and diesel options are fewer now. CNG: lowest fuel cost per km among combustion cars, ideal for high city running, but smaller boot (cylinder) and fewer refuelling stations in some areas. Electric: lowest running cost and very smooth, best with home charging and predictable daily distances. Strong hybrid: excellent city efficiency without charging, at a higher price.

## Car body types in India: hatchback, sedan, compact SUV, SUV, MUV/MPV
Hatchbacks are compact and easy to park. Sedans have a separate boot and comfortable rear seats. Compact or sub-compact SUVs are under 4 metres long (cheaper due to lower tax for sub-4m cars with small engines) and offer higher seating and ground clearance. Mid-size and full-size SUVs are larger and more powerful. MUVs/MPVs (e.g. 7-8 seaters) prioritise space and a usable third row for large families.

## Turbo-petrol vs naturally aspirated petrol
A turbocharged petrol engine uses exhaust gases to force more air into the engine, giving more power and torque from a smaller displacement, which makes the car feel punchier on highways. Naturally aspirated engines are simpler, smoother at low speeds and often more efficient in gentle city use. Turbo engines can be thirstier when driven hard.

## Car servicing basics in India
Most petrol and diesel cars need a periodic service every 10,000 km or 12 months (whichever comes first), with free services during the initial period; the exact schedule and cost depend on the manufacturer, model and city, so check the owner's manual or an authorised dealer. Standard warranties are commonly 2-3 years or 40,000-1,00,000 km, often extendable. EVs and strong hybrids usually have lower routine maintenance and longer battery warranties (often 8 years). CarWale model pages list warranty details where available, but not scheduled service costs.

## Connected car technology
Connected car features use an embedded SIM (eSIM) and a companion smartphone app to offer remote functions such as remote engine/AC start, door lock/unlock, vehicle location tracking, geo-fencing, speed alerts, trip analytics, emergency/SOS calls and stolen-vehicle tracking. Some cars also get over-the-air (OTA) software updates. These are often what buyers mean by a "smart" or "tech-loaded" car, along with large touchscreens, voice commands and ADAS.

## Key safety features to look for
Beyond airbags (dual front airbags are mandatory in India; many cars now offer six), look for ESC/ESP (electronic stability control), ABS with EBD, hill hold assist, ISOFIX child-seat mounts, three-point seatbelts for all passengers, TPMS (tyre pressure monitoring), rear parking camera/sensors and, in costlier cars, ADAS. A strong NCAP crash-test rating is the best overall indicator of crash safety.

## Drivetrain: FWD, RWD, AWD and 4x4
Front-wheel drive (FWD) is the most common, efficient and fine for normal roads. Rear-wheel drive (RWD) is found in some SUVs and performance cars. All-wheel drive (AWD) sends power to all wheels automatically for better grip in rain or on loose surfaces. 4x4 (four-wheel drive with low-range gearing) is meant for serious off-roading; it is only needed for rough terrain, hills or snow.

## Sunroof vs panoramic sunroof
A regular (single-pane) sunroof is a small openable glass panel above the front seats. A panoramic sunroof is a large glass roof extending over front and rear seats, making the cabin feel airy; usually the front section opens. In hot climates, sunroofs add heat load, so a good sunshade helps.

## How to choose the right variant
Base variants are the cheapest but often lack key features; top variants have everything but cost much more. The best value is usually a mid or upper-mid variant that adds important safety and convenience features (6 airbags, ESC, touchscreen with Android Auto/Apple CarPlay, rear camera) without the premium top-end extras. Compare the feature list across variants and decide which features you really need.
